## Quality priorities

Prioritize, in order:

1. Match the user's intention and hard facts.
2. Write natural, intelligible, singable language.
3. Give every section a clear narrative and emotional function.
4. Create a memorable central hook.
5. Use concrete scenes, actions, people, and objects.
6. Coordinate rhythm and rhyme.
7. Add literary polish without obscuring meaning.

Never sacrifice meaning or natural word order merely to rhyme. Avoid producing abstract “lyric-like” language with no identifiable person, action, place, or change.

## Build the song blueprint

Assign functions before writing:

- **Intro**: Establish atmosphere, motif, setting, or a brief opening voice.
- **Verse 1**: Establish person, relationship, place, time, or initiating event.
- **Pre-Chorus**: Move from story toward emotion and increase expectation.
- **Chorus**: State the central claim and carry the title or hook.
- **Verse 2**: Add new facts, time, contrast, consequence, or changed perspective.
- **Bridge**: Introduce a one-time realization, reversal, time jump, direct address, or emotional breakthrough.
- **Final Chorus**: Return to the hook with meaning changed by the preceding development.
- **Outro**: Resolve, echo the opening, or leave an intentional afterimage.

Do not add an empty section merely to complete a template. Do not use a repeated `Bridge` as the ordinary transition into each Chorus; label that function `Pre-Chorus`.

## Make abstract themes concrete

Translate concepts such as hope, regret, courage, love, future, or loneliness into sensory and relational evidence.

Give each song at least:

- One identifiable relationship or subject.
- One locatable scene.
- One concrete action.
- One recurring or returning image.
- One central hook.
- One legible emotional path.

Give each Verse at least one new fact and one concrete detail. Reuse common images such as stars, oceans, light, dreams, wings, distance, fireworks, or memories only when attached to a particular action, setting, relationship, or changed meaning.

Ask internally:

- Who is here?
- Where are they?
- What are they doing?
- What changes between sections?
- Which line should a listener remember?
- What does Verse 2 reveal that Verse 1 did not?

## Preserve singability

Count sung syllables rather than visual width. Most Chinese characters count as one syllable; read English words, numbers, and abbreviations by their actual pronunciation. Punctuation and spaces do not count and cannot repair an irregular line.

Choose a line grid before drafting each section. Common Mandarin-pop starting points include `8/8/8/8`, `7/7/8/8`, or another simple repeated pair—not one universal target for the whole song. Keep paired lines within one sung syllable where possible; allow a two-syllable difference only for a deliberate pickup, held note, or spoken fragment. Keep parallel Chorus lines within one syllable and preserve the exact grid when the hook repeats.

Use roughly 6–14 Chinese characters per line only as an outer drafting heuristic. Regularity inside the chosen section matters more than merely falling inside that wide range. Rap, spoken word, opera, folk, and experimental forms may use different prosodic logic, but they still need an intentional bar or phrase pattern.

Break lines at complete phrases, grammatical rests, emotional emphasis, or rhyme points. Avoid splitting a tight phrase unnaturally. Place semantic keywords near line endings, Chorus openings, refrain endings, or likely sustained notes.

Avoid:

- Consecutive overlong lines without breath points.
- Dense proper nouns, numbers, abbreviations, or tongue-twisting consonant clusters.
- Reversed syntax created only for rhyme.
- A Chorus that is denser and harder to remember than every Verse.
- Prose cut into short lines without rhythmic grouping.

## Handle rhyme

Use this priority:

```text
semantic accuracy > natural language > rhythmic flow > rhyme regularity
```

Plan rhyme by section before drafting. In Mandarin, judge sung rhyme mainly from the line-ending final—the main vowel and any nasal ending—while treating tone as secondary. Write down the intended family and candidate cadence words instead of discovering the rhyme accidentally after the section is complete.

- Keep Verse rhyme flexible enough for narration, but use a legible `AABB`, `ABAB`, or coherent two-family plan rather than unrelated endings.
- Let a two-line Pre-Chorus normally land `AA`, or use one clearly intentional pivot into the Chorus.
- Give the Chorus the strongest agreement: usually three of four or all four cadence lines should share a compatible family, depending on the form.
- Permit a Bridge to change family when the perspective changes.
- Treat the final content line of every section as the cadence anchor. If it accidentally falls outside the plan, rewrite it unless the rupture is intentional and supported by a pause or structural turn.

Avoid filling rhyme positions with generic words such as “远方、光芒、方向、翅膀” unless they carry specific meaning. Do not force inversion, vague filler, or inaccurate wording to obtain a rhyme. If meaning and rhyme conflict, first look for a natural synonymous ending or rebuild the whole phrase rather than attaching a disposable rhyme word.

Treat English words as semantic and rhythmic material, not decorative rhyme tokens.

## Build the prosody blueprint

Before writing prose-like lines, make a compact internal worksheet:

| Section | Sung-syllable grid | Rhyme plan | Cadence words/finals | Energy/register ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Verse 1 | `8/8/8/8` | `AABB` | planned before drafting | low-to-mid, intimate |
| Pre-Chorus | `7/7` | `AA` | compatible pair | restrained, no lift |
| Chorus | `9/9/9/9` | `AAAA` or `AAXA` | hook-led family | same register, deeper not louder |

The numbers and schemes are examples, not mandatory templates. Select them from the intended groove, speech rhythm, and reference analysis. Keep Verse 2 metrically compatible with Verse 1 when they are expected to share a melody. Keep repeated Choruses identical unless a changed final line is a deliberate compositional event.

Audit in this order after drafting:

1. Remove tags and count sung syllables line by line.
2. Mark the final and rhyme family of every cadence line.
3. Repair lines that break the grid or rhyme plan while preserving natural syntax and concrete meaning.
4. Re-read aloud for breath, stress, pronunciation, and hook recall.

## Design the hook

Build a hook that is short, speakable, repeatable, and representative of the whole song. Derive it from a title, conversational phrase, question, contrast, number, time, place, name, slogan, or repeated syntax.

Make the hook:

- Understandable on first hearing.
- Easy to pronounce.
- Capable of standing outside the full lyric.
- Naturally repeated, usually within the Chorus.
- Distinct from generic inspirational language.

Use stronger repetition for advertising, children's songs, chants, or event themes. Use subtler repetition for private, tragic, or narrative songs. Do not repeat the same hook on every line unless the form intentionally requires it.

## Apply edit authority

- **L0**: Add only structure, spacing, punctuation needed for presentation, and metatags. Do not change lyric words or order.
- **L1**: Fix local awkwardness, minor rhythm, redundant wording, and limited rhyme. Preserve theme, story, viewpoint, core lines, images, and facts.
- **L2**: Reorder sections, rewrite lines, add transitions, strengthen Chorus and Hook, and create a Bridge when useful. Preserve the core premise, relationships, facts, and protected text.
- **L3**: Rebuild narrative, structure, hook, and wording. Preserve only explicitly protected concepts, facts, brand language, or phrases.

Use L1 when a user only says “优化一下.” State the level when changes may surprise the user.

Never silently change names, brands, dates, numbers, claims, relationships, viewpoint, true experiences, required phrases, or forbidden content.

## Run the Chinese human-writing creation pass

For Chinese lyrics, read [chinese-human-lyrics.md](chinese-human-lyrics.md) before drafting. Use its method to make the narrator, scenes, syntax, and emotional reactions feel lived-in while preserving musical economy.

Build a small evidence bank first:

- Truthful facts and user-supplied personal details.
- One setting with sensory evidence.
- Two or three ordinary objects or actions that belong to that setting.
- One bodily reaction, hesitation, contradiction, or behavior that reveals emotion without naming it.
- One conversational phrase that could become the hook.
- One early image that can return with changed meaning near the end.

If the song is presented as autobiographical and a necessary personal detail is missing, ask for it or stay general; do not invent a fake memory. If the song is explicitly fictional, invent internally consistent details without attributing them to the user.

Draft through a scene chain rather than a stack of emotion labels:

```text
Verse 1: observable moment → small action → unspoken tension
Pre-Chorus: body or behavior changes → withheld sentence approaches
Chorus: plainspoken central phrase → emotional consequence
Verse 2: new fact or later moment → earlier object gains new meaning
Outro/Final Chorus: callback with a changed verb, addressee, or outcome
```

Allow controlled human irregularity: a short fragment, omitted subject, self-correction, conversational question, or asymmetrical line may make the voice specific. Use it only where it improves character or timing. Do not add random mistakes, internet slang, filler particles, or fashionable colloquialisms merely to simulate a person.

## Run the Chinese lyric optimization pass

For Chinese lyric creation, rewrite, audit, and listening iteration, run the four-pass check in [chinese-human-lyrics.md](chinese-human-lyrics.md). Apply it within the authorized L0–L3 edit boundary.

When optimizing existing lyrics:

1. Lock facts, viewpoint, addressee, protected lines, and edit level.
2. Mark generic declarations, interchangeable imagery, report-like logic, symmetrical filler, forced inversion, and repeated information.
3. Preserve strong idiosyncratic lines even when they are slightly irregular; do not polish away the user's voice.
4. Replace an abstract line with evidence already present elsewhere before inventing new detail.
5. If a stronger repair requires a personal fact that is not supplied, offer a placeholder question or explain the missing detail instead of fabricating it.
6. Recheck the section syllable grid, stress, vowel openness, breath, rhyme family, cadence anchor, and hook recall after every language repair.

Human tone does not outrank song function. Keep a clear hook, sectional contrast, singable phrasing, and narrative advancement. Do not turn lyrics into chopped prose.

## Adapt special song types

### Brand, campus, park, or corporate theme song

Convert factual material into human scenes and experiences. Integrate the name or slogan naturally into the hook. Preserve approved claims and data exactly. Balance clarity and poetry; do not turn the entire song into brochure copy.

### Short advertisement song

Enter the subject quickly, keep the name clear, limit information, repeat a concise hook, and make the excerpt useful for short-form distribution.

### Narrative folk song

Let Verses carry details and events. Permit looser rhyme and a modest Chorus. Avoid forcing every section toward a stadium climax.

### Rap

Design bar grouping, internal rhyme, end rhyme, stress, density, and flow changes. Contrast Verse density with a simpler Hook. Do not turn prose into rap merely by inserting line breaks.

### Duet

Define character A, character B, shared lines, dialogue logic, vocal contrast, and final convergence. Give each character distinct knowledge or language rather than dividing identical text mechanically.

### Instrumental

Do not invent lyrical content. Design sections as musical events and ensure Style excludes unauthorized vocal, spoken, chant, choir, or sampled-voice elements according to the user's scope.

## Check the lyric

- Summarize the song in one sentence.
- Verify viewpoint and addressee consistency.
- Confirm that Verse 1 establishes and Verse 2 advances.
- Confirm that the Chorus carries one central message.
- Confirm that the title or hook appears naturally.
- Keep a Bridge only when it changes understanding or energy.
- Balance line lengths and breathing groups.
- Verify the written syllable grid rather than relying on visual similarity; paired lines normally differ by no more than one sung syllable.
- Mark each section's rhyme family and confirm that its final cadence line belongs to the plan.
- Remove forced rhyme, empty abstractions, and repeated information.
- Replace knowledge-like emotion labels with observable evidence where possible.
- Confirm that at least one line or image feels specific to this narrator and cannot move unchanged into an unrelated song.
- Confirm that conversational breaks and irregularities are purposeful and singable, not random noise.
- Confirm that the ending recalls or transforms an earlier detail when the song's form benefits from closure.
- Preserve every hard constraint.
